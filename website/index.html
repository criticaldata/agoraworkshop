MIT CRITICAL DATA · AGORA

What happens when AI agents have to think together?

AGORA is a research program for observing how groups of language-model agents form judgments, influence one another, preserve disagreement, and turn many positions into a collective output.

01 / WHAT IS AGORA

Single-model tests miss what happens between models.

Most AI evaluation asks one model to answer one question. AGORA studies a different object: the group. It creates controlled workshops in which agents speak, respond, change position, write together, and leave a complete record of how the result emerged.

The first workshop is a contained experiment on the way to a larger simulation of academic publishing. It lets the team study interaction before prestige, peer review, editorial roles, and institutional incentives are added.

01

Group behaviour

Observe patterns that do not exist in an isolated response: herding, role formation, majority influence, and collective bias.

02

Visible process

Keep the discussion, private positions, report revisions, model versions, prompts, and failures connected.

03

Repeatable conditions

Freeze the protocol so another run — and another team — can test the same research question.

02 / RESEARCH QUESTIONS

Agreement is an outcome. It is not an explanation.

A group can end at the same answer through very different social processes.

01

When agents agree, what moved them?

Did an argument persuade them, did they accommodate the group while keeping a reservation, or did a minority position simply disappear?

02

Does group composition change the discussion?

AGORA compares three agents from one model family with a mixed panel drawn from different model families.

03

Does a required report reshape the conversation?

The pilot tests whether anticipating a joint output changes position movement, participation, and the survival of dissent.

04

What survives into the collective output?

The final report is matched back to the transcript: proposals introduced, revised, retained, or omitted.

03 / THE PILOT

Three agents. Two variables. Twelve workshops.

The feasibility pilot asks whether the protocol can distinguish interaction effects before the study grows.

04 / HOW A WORKSHOP RUNS

The same sequence, every time.

Scheduling and visibility are controlled by ordinary program code. The agents determine the content of the conversation, not the protocol around it.

T0

Private baseline

Each agent answers the three questions alone. These answers never enter the shared history.

→
R1–R2

Human problems

Agents propose limitations and respond to one another.

→
R3–R4

Possible redesigns

The group discusses changes to capabilities, behaviour, or institutions.

→
R5–R6

Role of agents

Participants consider what role AI systems should have in those changes.

→
T6

Private position

Each agent answers alone again before any report is written.

→
TR*

Report and review

In report conditions: draft, two reviews, one revision, then a final private position.

* The report workflow exists only in conditions B and D. Discussion always stops after round six, whether or not the group agrees.

05 / WHAT AGORA MEASURES

Follow a position, not just the final sentence.

01 / PERSUASION

An argument changes the position.

The public statement moves after an argument, and the private position moves with it.

02 / ACCOMMODATION

The public compromise hides a reservation.

The agent agrees with the group in public while its private statement preserves a qualification.

03 / OMISSION

A proposal disappears without being withdrawn.

The transcript contains the position, but the shared report does not.

DURING THE WORKSHOP

Participation and position change

Word share, proposals, responses, speaking order, refusals, directive language, and public/private movement are recorded by participant and round.

AFTER THE WORKSHOP

Context-locked replay

Selected changes are replayed from the same prior context with identity and endorsement cues present or removed, separating the argument from the voice that carried it.

06 / RESEARCH PROGRAMME

The workshop is the first contained experiment, not the endpoint.

AGORA is being developed toward a multi-agent simulation of academic publishing: a setting where prestige, review, editorial power, and collective judgment can be studied as a system.

NOW / FEASIBILITY PILOT

Composition × report requirement

MIT CRITICAL DATA · AGORA

What happens when AI agents have to think together?

AGORA is a research program for observing how groups of language-model agents influence one another, form collective judgments, and turn many voices into shared outcomes.

01 / WHAT IS AGORA

A laboratory for the social life of AI agents.

Most AI evaluation looks at one model at a time. AGORA studies what appears only when models interact.

In controlled workshops, agents exchange ideas, respond to disagreement, and produce collective outputs. The result is a visible record of how the group arrived where it did.

01

Observe interaction

See how agents influence one another and how group behaviour differs from isolated responses.

02

Keep disagreement visible

Follow which ideas survive, change, or disappear as the group moves toward an outcome.

03

Make research repeatable

Run comparable workshops under clearly defined conditions.

02 / WHY IT MATTERS

The final answer hides the most interesting part.

When a group agrees, the output alone cannot tell us why.

01

Influence

Was a position changed by a strong argument, a majority, a model identity, or the pressure to agree?

02

Diversity

Do mixed groups preserve more perspectives, or do they still converge on the same ideas?

03

Representation

Which voices make it into a shared output — and which ones quietly disappear?

03 / HOW IT WORKS

Put agents in a room. Follow what changes.

AGORA turns interaction into something that can be observed, compared, and studied.

04 / RESEARCH DIRECTIONS

From small workshops to artificial institutions.

The first studies focus on deliberation. The larger program asks how agent societies behave when roles, incentives, reputation, and governance enter the room.

NOW

Collective judgment

How groups form shared positions, preserve alternatives, and respond to disagreement.

NEXT

Identity and influence

How model family, roles, moderation, memory, and social cues shape the group.

LONG TERM

Artificial institutions

How multi-agent systems behave inside peer review, publishing, and other collective decision processes.

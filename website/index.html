A mirror for how science gets made.

AGORA studies how AI agents influence one another, how ideas change through interaction, and how shared rules shape collective outcomes. Our first workshop pilot opens a window onto these dynamics; the wider research ambition extends to academic publishing.

01 / WHAT IS AGORA

Simulate academia to understand ourselves.

Our long-term ambition is a connected simulation of authors, reviewers, editors, journals, and reputations. The workshop provides a contained starting point for studying how interactions shape the knowledge a group produces.

The aim is not to automate scientific discovery. It is to use an artificial publishing world as a mirror for the human one.

01

Recreate the ecosystem

Model authors, journals, review, publication, citations, and reputation as one social system.

02

Watch norms emerge

Observe prestige bias, herding, gatekeeping, and strategies that were never explicitly programmed.

03

Compare possible worlds

Change the rules, then see how a different institution changes what gets rewarded.

EXPLORATORY OBSERVATIONS

What we observe

Initial reading has identified concerns that disappear from final reports, ideas that become narrower, and preferences whose reasoning is not fully carried forward.

A polished report can suggest agreement while leaving unresolved objections behind. These are examples to validate, not established rates or causal findings.

VALIDATION PENDING

How we evaluate

We follow proposals and objections through the discussion and compare their final status with the report. The pilot varies model composition and reporting conditions.

Independent annotators will apply a shared codebook after calibration, recording source evidence and agreement between raters. Validated behavioural scores are not yet available.

MITIGATION UNDER STUDY

What we can correct

We explored giving reports more space after short or truncated outputs. Some regenerated reports improved on inspection, but omissions persisted and other changes limit attribution.

We have not demonstrated a general fix for lost objections, conformity, or misleading consensus. Regenerated reports remain separate from the primary evidence.

From visible failures to tested interventions.

Next: complete independent annotation, establish a validated baseline, then test whether a specific change reduces information loss in a controlled comparison.

02 / WHY THE WORKSHOP

Put agents together. See what emerges.

Some risks exist only between agents. The workshop creates a controlled, auditable room where different models can influence one another without a human steering each turn.

01

Emergence

What behaviours appear only once agents can communicate, imitate, and coordinate?

02

Goal pressure

Do they solve the task, game the reward, exploit a shortcut, or redefine the goal?

03

Accountability

Can we reconstruct how the group reached an unexpected or dangerous outcome?

03 / HOW IT WORKS

Configure. Trace. Compare.

The workshop makes the interaction process inspectable. Its records connect experimental conditions, discussion turns, and report production.

01

Configure

Specify participants, models, information access, and reporting conditions in a versioned protocol.

04 / RESEARCH DIRECTIONS

Study the system, not just the model.

Beyond the workshop pilot, the research agenda asks how roles, incentives, and institutions shape collective behaviour. These directions describe future experiments, not completed findings.

01

Peer review

Trace how scientific concerns are formed, transferred, challenged, lost, or amplified.

02

Counterfactual worlds

Compare different incentive rules without asking a real journal to experiment on itself.

03

Institutional reflection

Use the artificial ecosystem to make the habits of human publishing easier to see and question.

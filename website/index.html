A mirror for how science gets made.

AGORA is building an agentic simulation of academic publishing. By watching AI authors, reviewers, editors, and journals form a working ecosystem, we can study how prestige, incentives, and institutional rules shape knowledge.

Discover AGORA ↓View on GitHub ↗

01 / WHAT IS AGORA

Simulate academia to understand ourselves.

Agents analyse public data, write papers, submit them, review one another, revise, publish, cite, and build reputations. Every step becomes an observable record.

The aim is not to automate scientific discovery. It is to use an artificial publishing world as a mirror for the human one.

01

Recreate the ecosystem

Model authors, journals, review, publication, citations, and reputation as one social system.

02

Watch norms emerge

Observe prestige bias, herding, gatekeeping, and strategies that were never explicitly programmed.

03

Compare possible worlds

Change the rules, then see how a different institution changes what gets rewarded.

02 / WHY IT MATTERS

Scientific knowledge is shaped by more than evidence.

Journals, incentives, reputations, and review structures affect what is seen, trusted, and rewarded.

01

Prestige

Does an author’s reputation change how the same work is judged?

02

Incentives

What happens when a system rewards novelty, volume, replication, or attention?

03

Gatekeeping

Which ideas survive review, which disappear, and who gets to decide?

04 / RESEARCH DIRECTIONS

Study the system, not just the model.

A sociological and anthropological lens on how agents organise, compete, and evolve — and what their behaviour reveals about us.

01

Peer review

Trace how scientific concerns are formed, transferred, challenged, lost, or amplified.

02

Counterfactual worlds

Compare different incentive rules without asking a real journal to experiment on itself.

03

Institutional reflection

Use the artificial ecosystem to make the habits of human publishing easier to see and question.

Agentic Game of Research and Academia

What happens when AI agents work together?

Ideas evolve. Objections surface. A shared answer takes shape. AGORA studies the interactions behind that answer.

We bring AI agents into controlled discussions to understand how they challenge, influence and cooperate with one another.

Explore the approach ↓Talk to the team ↗

01 / THE IDEA

Make collective AI behavior observable.

Can agents challenge an idea, converge too quickly, or find ways of cooperating we didn’t program?

AGORA is a research program built around these questions. We preserve the exchanges that lead to a shared answer, so researchers can follow how a proposal changes and what gets left behind.

Explore the research documents ↗

THE LONGER-TERM AMBITION

Different rules.
Different possible worlds.

We want to simulate academic publishing: authors, reviewers, editors, and reputations. By changing the rules and incentives, we can study how institutions shape the knowledge a group produces.

Author→Reviewer→Editor

The workshop pilot is our starting point. The wider publishing ecosystem is a research ambition.

02 / OUR APPROACH

A discussion we can trace, step by step.

Our first pilot puts three AI participants into a workshop on human limitations, possible social changes, and the role of AI.

01

Start individually.

Collect each participant’s response before the group discussion, with the experimental conditions recorded.

02

Let ideas meet.

Run six public discussion rounds. Keep every turn so proposals and objections can be followed through the exchange.

123456
03

Follow what survives.

Collect individual responses again. In the report conditions, compare the final report with the discussion that produced it.

THE COMPARISON

Same-model and mixed-model groups, each with or without an announced report. Four conditions, with three workshops in each.

B3 / A PROPOSAL EVOLVES

From adjusting an assessment to flagging it for review.

“adjust its assessment accordingly”
↓
“flag cases where further human review is warranted”

A housing-assistance proposal narrowed during the discussion. Participants then questioned whether overloaded caseworkers could provide the oversight it needed.

In both versions of the proposal, human caseworkers retained final authority. The proposal changed, with important concerns still unresolved.

04 / WHAT COMES NEXT

Turn observations into evidence we can test.

Independent annotators will examine the records using shared criteria. Then we can compare conditions and test specific changes. We do not yet have validated effect sizes or a demonstrated general fix.
